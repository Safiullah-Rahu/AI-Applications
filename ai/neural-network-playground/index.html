How NeuroPlayground works

Everything here — the network, back-propagation, optimisers and visualisations — is ~400 lines of plain JavaScript, with gradients verified against finite differences.

Model

a⁽⁰⁾ = features(x₁, x₂) z⁽ˡ⁾ = W⁽ˡ⁾ a⁽ˡ⁻¹⁾ + b⁽ˡ⁾, a⁽ˡ⁾ = φ(z⁽ˡ⁾) ŷ = σ(z⁽ᴸ⁾), L = −[y log ŷ + (1 − y) log(1 − ŷ)] + (λ/2)‖W‖²

Hidden activations φ: ReLU, Leaky ReLU, tanh, sigmoid or GELU, with He / Xavier initialisation to match.

Back-propagation

δ⁽ᴸ⁾ = ŷ − y δ⁽ˡ⁾ = (W⁽ˡ⁺¹⁾ᵀ δ⁽ˡ⁺¹⁾) ⊙ φ′(z⁽ˡ⁾) ∂L/∂W⁽ˡ⁾ = δ⁽ˡ⁾ a⁽ˡ⁻¹⁾ᵀ

Optimisers

  • SGD: w ← w − η g
  • Momentum: v ← 0.9 v − η g, w ← w + v
  • Adam: bias-corrected first/second moment estimates, w ← w − η m̂ / (√v̂ + ε)

Visualisations

  • Every neuron shows its activation over the input square; edges are coloured by weight sign and thickness by magnitude.
  • The decision boundary plots ŷ(x₁, x₂); filled dots are training points, ringed dots are held-out test points.
  • Gradient flow plots the RMS gradient of each weight layer per epoch on a log scale. With deep sigmoid networks the early layers’ gradients collapse by orders of magnitude — the vanishing-gradient problem that ReLU, careful initialisation and adaptive optimisers mitigate.
