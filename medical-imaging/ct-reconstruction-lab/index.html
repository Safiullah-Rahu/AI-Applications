How TomoLab works

TomoLab reproduces the full imaging chain of a (parallel-beam) CT scanner: object → X-ray projections → noisy measurements → reconstruction → image-quality assessment.

1 · Phantoms & exact projections

Phantoms are built from ellipses and rectangles with attenuation values relative to water (HU = 1000·(μ/μwater − 1), μwater = 0.19 cm⁻¹). Their Radon transform is computed analytically — e.g. the chord of an ellipse:

p(θ, t) = ρ · 2ab·√(a²(θ) − s²) / a²(θ) a²(θ) = a²cos²(θ−α) + b²sin²(θ−α), s = t − (x₀cosθ + y₀sinθ)

so reconstructions are not flattered by the “inverse crime” of projecting the same pixel grid they are rebuilt on. Uploaded images use a ray-driven bilinear projector instead.

2 · Measurement physics

N ~ Poisson(I₀ · e^(−p)) p̂ = −ln(N / I₀)

Lower dose (I₀) means fewer photons and noisier line integrals — especially behind dense objects such as metal (photon starvation → streaks). Optional miscalibrated detector channels create ring artefacts; rigid patient motion half-way through the scan creates inconsistent data and streaks.

3 · Filtered back-projection

f(x, y) = Δθ · Σₖ (pₖ ∗ h)(x cosθₖ + y sinθₖ)

The ramp filter h is built from its exact band-limited spatial kernel (Kak & Slaney) and apodised by a Shepp–Logan, cosine, Hamming or Hann window to trade resolution for noise. Plain BP (no filter) shows the characteristic 1/r blur.

4 · Iterative reconstruction

OS-SART repeatedly corrects the image with the back-projected, normalised residual of 10 ordered view subsets:

x ← x + λ · Cₛ⁻¹ Aₛᵀ Rₛ⁻¹ (pₛ − Aₛx), x ≥ 0

SART-TV interleaves gradient steps on total variation (ASD-POCS style), a compressed-sensing prior that suppresses streaks when only a few views are available.

5 · Image quality

RMSE (HU), PSNR and SSIM are computed inside the circular field of view against the rasterised ground truth.

Things to try

  • Drop to 30 views: compare FBP streaks with SART-TV.
  • Lower the dose to 10³ and switch the filter from Ram-Lak to Hann.
  • Load the hip phantom — classic metal artefacts appear at clinical doses.
  • Restrict coverage to 120° for limited-angle artefacts.
